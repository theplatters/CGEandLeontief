\section{The pricing door: derivations, guard and accounting}\label{app:pricing-door}

\subsection{The pricing equation and the factor demands}\label{app:pricing-hooks}
Sector $i$ produces with the CES unit cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-unit-cost}
  c_i \;=\; \Bigl[\,(A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i)^{\epsilon-1}\bigl(\alpha_i\,w_i^{1-\epsilon}
     + (1-\alpha_i)\,P_i^{1-\epsilon}\bigr)\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}},
\end{equation}
where $P_i$ is the intermediate price index (CES in $\theta$ over the purchaser
prices $p_u$) and $\alpha_i$ the factor share. Both modifications introduced in Section~\ref{sec:beyond-demand-only} can be captured in this way ---
one shifts it, the other marks it up:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-price-rule}
  p_i \;=\; \mu_i\,c_i\bigl(A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mu_i \;=\; 1 + \kappa\Bigl(\frac{y_i}{\lambda_i} - 1\Bigr),
  \qquad
  A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i \;=\; A_i\Bigl(\frac{y_i}{\lambda_i}\Bigr)^{-\delta_i \alpha_i},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_i$ the sector's baseline gross output. The capacity modification keeps zero profit ($\mu_i \equiv 1$) and lets technology respond; the markup modification keeps $A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i = A_i$ and lets the margin respond. The factor share in the
exponent converts a value-added elasticity into a gross-output one: at fixed input
prices a Hicks-neutral change $d\ln A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i$ raises value added per worker
by $d\ln A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i/\alpha_i$, so value-added productivity responds to own
output with elasticity $-\delta_i$. Both modifications are written in $y_i/\lambda_i$, so at the baseline ($y = \lambda$)
both are no-ops --- technology is $A_i$ and the margin is one. That leaves the kernel's
scale-determinacy test untouched, since the test is evaluated at exactly that point, and
$\delta = \kappa = 0$ returns the demand-only model: every priced cell reproduces its
column of Table~\ref{tab:results-matrix}.

The firm, however, buys at the purchaser prices while it hires and buys inputs at
its \emph{marginal cost} $c_i = p_i/\mu_i$. Shephard's lemma on
\eqref{eq:app-unit-cost} then gives labour demand and input demand together,
\begin{align}
  L_i \;&=\; c_i^{\epsilon}\,(A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i)^{\epsilon-1}\,\alpha_i\,y_i\,w_i^{-\epsilon},
  \label{eq:app-labour}\\
  x_{ui} \;&=\; p_u^{-\theta}\,\Omega_{iu}\;c_i^{\epsilon}\,
     (A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i)^{\epsilon-1}\,P_i^{\theta-\epsilon}\,a_i\,y_i ,
  \label{eq:app-intermediate}
\end{align}
with $a_i$ the domestic intermediate bill per unit of output: the outer
$p_u^{-\theta}$ is the price of the demanded input and stays the purchaser price,
the inner $c_i^{\epsilon}$ is the user's marginal cost. The same factor
$k_i = c_i^{\epsilon}(A^{\mathrm{eff}}_i)^{\epsilon-1}P_i^{1-\epsilon}$ values the
two leaks of the purchaser-price intermediate bill, imported intermediates
$M^{\mathrm{int}}$ and product taxes on intermediate use $T^{\mathrm{int}}$. The
price therefore enters the first-order conditions at four places --- labour demand,
the inner factor of input demand, and both leak valuations --- and writing $p$
instead of $c$ at any of them inflates the corresponding demand by
$\mu^{\epsilon}$ while leaving the price response untouched. Under the capacity
modification $\mu \equiv 1$ so that $c = p$ and only $A^{\mathrm{eff}}$ moves.

\subsection{The uniqueness guard}\label{app:pricing-guard}
With $\delta_i < 0$ a sector's unit cost falls as its output rises, a positive
feedback that can admit several equilibria. Every priced cell is reached by a
deterministic continuation in its door parameter (four equal steps from the
door-free solution) and must pass a uniqueness gate: re-solved from four
starting points (the default initial point, the door-free solution, and the
solution with all quantities scaled by $0.9$ and $1.1$), at least two must
converge and every converged start must lie within $10^{-6}$ of the solution,
and the Jacobian of the full residual must have full rank. All priced cells pass the gate, on every labor closure.

\subsection{Accounting with the profit booked}\label{app:pricing-accounting}
With total profits $\Pi$ of equation~\eqref{eq:profit-bill} and a retention rate
$s_\pi$, the household spends $E = (1-\tau)\,w\textstyle\sum_i L_i + F + (1-s_\pi)\Pi$, and the external-account identity of the kernel becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-account}
  sE + s_\pi \Pi + T^{\mathrm{int}} + M - (I + X) \;=\; F + B_{\mathrm{gov}},
\end{equation}
which every priced run satisfies to $10^{-11}$; dropping the $s_\pi\Pi$ term leaves it
short by exactly the retained profit.

On the income side GDP is factor income $w\sum_i L_i + \Pi$, so the companion identity
between the GDP wedge and the account gap holds unchanged. Full retention ($s_\pi = 1$)
is admissible only because the application carries injections --- government demand,
investment and exports; without them the same identity admits no solution with positive
output.

\subsection{Robustness: the all-sector capacity profile}\label{app:profiles}

In Section~\ref{subsec:pricing-capacity} the utilization elasticity is confined to
the goods sectors, because the entire direct incidence of the program falls
there and because \textcite{Kaldor1966} reads Verdoorn's law as a law of
manufacturing. As a robustness check, Table~\ref{tab:pricing-profiles} reports the
alternative in which $\delta$ extends to every sector.

Where the elasticity sits relative to the demand decides what it does. Confined to
the goods sectors it meets the program's direct demand --- the sectors that expand are
the sectors whose unit costs fall --- and in the closures whose price level is anchored
by the wage the deflator moves by at most $0.06$ percent, in the direction the
coefficient implies (Table~\ref{tab:pricing-kaldor}). Spread over the 36 service
sectors as well, the same coefficient acts where no program demand arrives directly:
their output moves only as far as the financing closure reallocates demand away from
them or adds income to them, and there the deflator stops following the coefficient.
Under $\delta = -0.5$ it falls by $0.20$ and $0.16$ percent under preference
reallocation and tax finance and rises by $0.12$ percent under external finance; the
same reversal appears in the Leontief closure ($-0.16$, $-0.13$ and $+0.14$ percent),
while the standard CGE closure lowers the deflator by 0.16 percent in every column.
The all-sector profile is therefore a statement about where returns are assumed to
live, not about the size of the coefficient.

In the three closures whose price level is set by the CPI (no-reallocation,
standard CGE, elastic supply) F2 and F3 stay indistinguishable in every row, so
the transfer neutrality behind \eqref{eq:finstrument} survives the pricing
door.
