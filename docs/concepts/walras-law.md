# Walras' Law

Walras' Law holds that in a general-equilibrium model the excess demands of all markets sum to zero, so one market-clearing condition is implied by the remaining ones and the absolute price level is not determined by the equilibrium. The Beyond Hulten note states the operational form used in this project: "The engine is one excess-demand equation per market; because [[Walras' Law]] holds identically, the sector whose equation I throw away is fixed automatically — its excess demand must be zero if the others are — so I solve n−1 equations and pin the price level with a numeraire."[^1] The Shoven & Whalley survey states the companion price-level fact that "only relative prices are of any significance in such a model; the absolute price level has no impact on the equilibrium outcome."[^2]

In the kernel the price level is pinned by the numeraire CPI = 1, imposed by dropping a price/zero-profit equation and adding the CPI constraint; the CGE concept doc records that this redundancy is valid only when the household budget constraint is actually enforced, and that the point is load-bearing for the mobile-labour implementation.[^3] The redundancy is therefore conditional rather than automatic: Walras' law recovers the omitted equation only out of a system whose household budget balances.

The pre-fix mobile-labour implementation violated that precondition. `src/mobile_labor.jl:148` applied the demand shock as an unnormalized household expenditure shifter, so shock-weighted household expenditure exceeded income — 25.5% in the two-sector diagnostic and 4.5% in the independently reproduced 71-sector model — and Walras' law could no longer recover the omitted sector-1 zero-profit condition, which carried a residual of 10.924; the model was accordingly not an equilibrium.[^4][^5] The repair restored the dropped sector-1 zero-profit equation and normalized household final demand to income, after which residuals fell to machine precision for η = 0…50.[^6]

The same evidence fixes the validation rule for any solve that omits a market: all N goods-market residuals must be computed, including the one left out of the system, and the diagnostic repeated while omitting each commodity market in turn, because real allocations should be invariant to which market is thrown away. A solution counts as valid only when every zero-profit, goods-market, labour-market and institutional-budget residual is small and the solver reports successful termination.[^5] The sticky-wage (`:fixed`) closure handles the numeraire from the other side, hard-coding w = 1.0 and solving a 2N system for prices and quantities only instead of carrying a wage unknown with a linear constraint that produced a near-singular Jacobian.[^6]
