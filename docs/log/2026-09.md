**Specification (derived, then validated before any `src/` change).** At
eta = 0 the sectoral allocation stays frozen at `data.labor_share` and the
single economy-wide wage is replaced by a wage per sector, set by that
sector's own marginal product at the frozen allocation. Unknowns

equation by N sectoral conditions adds N - 1 equations, so the block has one
equation more than directions to pin and the demand block needs one free
scalar. The alternative — dropping one clearing equation — is the ADR-0010

## 2026-09-18 (cont.) — GAMMA: can the apparatus generate variation? (ADR-0021 proposed)

**The question.** GAMMA carries one degree of freedom in the matrix: F2 and F3
are real-neutral by theorem and DELTA reproduces GAMMA to six digits, so the
three GAMMA cells are effectively two economies. Can the ADR-0020 apparatus
(the vector-wage path) generate variation there?

**The diagnosis.** The flatness is a property of the design, not of the model
class: the closure's parameter space already contains a wage vector, the design
sets it to its degenerate value (all sectors equal) and the kernel hard-codes
that value as the scalar `w = 1`.

**The measurement** (`experiments/probes/probe11_gamma_wage_structure.jl`,
`GAMMA-F2` cell, full-71 A-bill; the residual mirrors `problem_fixed` with the
scalar pin replaced by `wbar`):

- The degenerate pin reproduces the kernel cell: residual 8.9e-16, employment
  1.00125981.
- The wage LEVEL is not an instrument: `wbar = 0.9` and `1.1` leave the
  allocation, employment and real income invariant to 8.9e-16, with the CPI
  scaling exactly (0.9 / 1.1). This follows from the block's homogeneity of
  degree zero in (p, w) and holds because the model has no nominal anchor
  besides the wage -- a condition that belongs in any sentence citing it.
- The wage STRUCTURE is an instrument: a ten percent wage push in the seven
  programme sectors moves employment by about half a percent (1.00686452
  against 1.00125981) and real income by about one percent; a single-sector
  tilt moves the allocation by about two percent in log terms; a broad tilt
  (sectors 1 to 5) by about 3.5 percent. These exceed GAMMA's entire existing
  F1 to F2 spread, and the parameter is continuous.
- The decisive claim, previously unmeasured, is now measured: the external
  account closes under every tilt. The price-weighted clearing residual stays
  at the solver-residual level (2.9e-16 at the degenerate pin, at most 7.0e-12
  under a tilt), and it equals the kernel canary's `diff` exactly along
  quantity perturbations (ratio 1.0000 at one and ten per mille), so the proxy
  IS the gap; it was validated off equilibrium because both quantities are
  machine zero on it. The price-perturbation case gives -1.95, i.e. the
  relation is an equilibrium statement, not a universal algebraic identity.
- Conditioning is unaffected: `cond(J)` between 6.32 and 6.34 across all
  scenarios, and the ADR-0017 admission criterion never involves the wage.

**Two corrections to earlier wording.** First, the fixed-wage system is NOT
near-singular at this calibration (`cond(J)` = 6.3), so the GAMMA/DELTA 1e-11
metric sensitivity to the warm start is a measurement-layer amplification of
the solver's residual level, not a singular solve; the "near-singular" phrasing
used in the v6 documents was corrected in place. Second, the canary's 2N branch
pins `w = 1`, which is why the probe had to measure the identity through the
price-weighted clearing residual rather than through the kernel's own gate.

**What is proposed, not done.** ADR-0021 (proposed) would make the pinned wage
vector a parameter of the existing fixed-wage closure (default: the current
scalar pin, so every existing cell is bit-identical), with the wage structure as
a preregistered design dimension rather than a new closure. It needs a `src/`
change, the canary and `gdp_components` extended to the vector, and a new
generation for provenance (ADR-0004). The scenario set is the one open decision
left to the operator; the zero-cost alternative, which needs no kernel change,
is a sectoral supply-shock design. Options, evidence and the claim-by-claim
provenance are in `docs/VariationinGamma.md` (Version 2).
