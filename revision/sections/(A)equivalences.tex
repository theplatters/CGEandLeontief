\section{Equivalences and overlaps of the closure matrix}\label{app:equivalences}

\noindent
Section~\ref{sec:two-questions} observes that several of the five
labour-market closures coincide at characteristic parameter values and
that, under the demand-only shock of our application. Correspondinly,
of several cells of the evaluation matrix (\ref{tab:results-matrix} in section \ref{sec:application}) describe the same economy. This appendix states the
formal reasons for the observed coincidences and names the mechanism behind 
each (an exact identity, a limit, or a definitional inclusion). Moreover, it covers those overlaps that are mentioned in description of our conceptual setup in section \ref{sec:two-questions}. More exact derivations that provide further details to the exposition here are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Key coincidences on the closure matrix}\label{app:eqpairs}

\paragraph{Fixed real wage $\equiv$ Leontief.}
With the real wage pegged, $(p,y)=(\one,y^{\ast})$ solves the system at
every elasticity triple, and the Leontief corner selects the same candidate
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gd}). The equivalence is exact because under a demand-only shock a fixed wage already pins prices: the substitution elasticities $(\theta,\epsilon,
\sigma)$ have nothing left to move. In the fixed-wage gauge they are
unidentified. The coincidence is definitional in \emph{form} (the Leontief
corner is the fixed-wage closure evaluated at the zero-substitution limit)
but substantive in mechanism leading to a synchronized response of the fixed wage models.

\paragraph{The financing column.} In the flexible-wage rows the external transfer
$R$ is exactly the instrument that keeps the labour at place, and the
two additive financings differ by a shift in $R$ that leaves the real
allocation untouched: $R_{F3}=R_{F2}-B_{\mathrm{gov}}$. Hence, so F2 and F3
coincide and the financing label is unidentified (Section~\ref{sec:gauge}). The fixed-wage rows are the exception: there $R$ is pinned at zero and the labour pin is absent, so financing \emph{does} bite --- the F2/F3 spread in the fixed-wage row is the one difference the financing column carries.

\subsection{Key overlaps outside the closure matrix}\label{app:eqpairs}

\paragraph{Standard CGE $\equiv$ single-wage elastic supply $\equiv$ Full rellocation.}
The supply equation $\sum_i L_i=\Lbar\,(w/\Pi)^{\eta_s}$ and full
employment $\sum_i L_i=\Lbar$ are the same equation once the real-wage
anchor binds: for \emph{every} $\eta_s$, and under every financing closure,
the two systems coincide equation by equation (Proposition~\ref{prop:ab}).
The single-wage supply elasticity $\eta_s$ is therefore unidentified on the
demand-only slice. The coincidence is \emph{derivable} from the anchor; it
is not definitional. Similarly, the only difference between the no-reallocation model and the standard CGE is the assumption of labor market rigidity across sectors.

\paragraph{No reallocation $\equiv$ the rigid corner of the sectoral
family.} A frozen allocation of workers across sectors ($\eta=0$ in the no-reallocation model) and an infinitely inelastic supply curve lead to the same practical implications.
In term of algebra this implies that setting $\eta_{s,i}=0$ for all $i$ reproduces the
$\eta=0$ endpoint (Proposition~\ref{prop:nest}). This one is exact by
construction; again it equates two \emph{closures} rather than two executed cells, and it is why no separate rigid sectoral cell is reported.

\subsection{Scope}\label{app:eqscope}

Three features of the kernel are relevant for contextualizing the coincidences and overlaps described above.

\paragraph{A demand-only shock.} The programme enters final demand at baseline
technology ($A\equiv\one$), so the shock touches neither the price nor the
technology margin. This is the demand-only slice on which the whole matrix
is evaluated; it is the case of our application.

\paragraph{A price block that cannot see demand.} The zero-profit conditions are
homogeneous of degree one in $(p,w)$ and contain no demand variable, so
relative prices are pinned by technology alone (Lemma~\ref{lem:price}).
Under the consumption-price numeraire the real wage then sits at its
baseline anchor $w/\Pi(p)=1$ for every demand vector, every financing
closure and every shock size (Corollary~\ref{cor:realwage}). A closure
whose only task is to move the \emph{wage} margin is thus inert: the margin
it is meant to clear is already at rest.

\paragraph{A quantity block that is elasticity-free.} At those prices the
clearing system is exactly affine and independent of $(\theta,\epsilon,
\sigma)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}) --- the production side of the Samuelson
nonsubstitution theorem (Remark~\ref{rem:samuelson}). Every closure that
pegs the real wage therefore solves one and the same linear system,
whatever the technology.
