\caption{The closure matrix, transposed: the three financing closures
(rows) crossed with the five labour-market closures (columns). Each cell
states the labour-market condition (the column), any production-side
condition (only the Leontief column pins substitution), and the financing
condition (the row). Common to all cells: household expenditure is
$E^{h} = (1-\tau)\,w\sum_i L_i + F$, where $\tau$ is the wage-income tax rate
that finances the baseline government purchases $g^{G}_i$ in every closure
(and the programme $g_i$ under F2), $F$ is the net external transfer closing
$S + T_{\mathrm{int}} + M - (I+X) = F + B_{\mathrm{gov}}$, endogenous on the
mobile labour rows and identically zero on the fixed-wage rows, and
$B_{\mathrm{gov}} = \sum_i p_i g_i$ is the programme booked externally (F3 only;
zero otherwise). The programme enters final demand as additive real demand
$g_i$ under F2 and F3; under F1 it is a reallocation of the household preference
weights $\tilde\beta_i \propto \beta_i d_i$. $\theta$, $\epsilon$ and $\sigma$ are
the substitution elasticities whose limit $0^{+}$ gives fixed coefficients.
The orientation matches Table~\ref{tab:closure-conditions}.}
