\section{One kernel, five closures, three financings}\label{sec:kernel}

% TODO: verbal model overview BEFORE the equations (R1.2).

All fifteen economies of the matrix share one static kernel: a production
network calibrated to an input--output table, cost-minimizing producers and
a utility-maximizing household. The kernel is held fixed by design; the
closures change only which variables adjust. This section states the kernel
in its most minimal form; functional forms and the closure equations are in
Appendix~\ref{app:model}.

\subsection{The input--output table}\label{subsec:io-table}

An input--output table records, for $N$ sectors, the deliveries $x_{ui}$ of
good $u$ to sector $i$, final use $z_u$ of good $u$ (household consumption,
government purchases, investment, exports) and the value added $v_i$ of
each sector. Two accounting identities hold for every sector. Along a row,
output equals intermediate and final use; down a column, revenue equals
the cost of intermediate inputs plus value added:
\begin{align}
p_u y_u &= \sum_{i=1}^N p_u x_{ui} + p_u z_u,
\label{eq:io-row}\\
p_i y_i &= \sum_{u=1}^N p_u x_{ui} + v_i.
\label{eq:io-column}
\end{align}
Dividing a column by sector revenue turns the table into cost shares: the
labour share $\alpha_i = v_i/(p_i y_i)$ and the input shares
$\Omega_{iu} = p_u x_{ui}/\sum_{u'} p_{u'}x_{u'i}$ of the intermediate
bundle, with $\sum_u \Omega_{iu} = 1$. Household consumption shares
$\omega_i$ follow in the same way from the final-use columns. Imports and
product taxes on intermediate use are booked as separate leaks
(Appendix~\ref{app:kernel}).

The table is a snapshot: it fixes shares at one point, but says nothing
about how they respond when prices or demand change. That is the job of the
optimization problems below.

\subsection{The optimization problems}\label{subsec:optimization}

Each sector is a single competitive firm with a constant-returns technology
$\Phi_i$ in labour and intermediate inputs. Given prices $p$, its wage $w_i$
and output $y_i$, it minimizes cost:
\begin{equation}
\min_{L_i,\,x_{1i},\ldots,x_{Ni}} \; w_i L_i + \sum_{u=1}^N p_u x_{ui}
\qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad \Phi_i(L_i, x_{1i},\ldots,x_{Ni}; A_i) \geq y_i,
\label{eq:cost-min}
\end{equation}
where $A_i$ is productivity. Constant returns make total cost
$c_i(p,w_i;A_i)\,y_i$, with unit cost $c_i$, and perfect competition
equates price and unit cost, $p_i = c_i(p,w_i;A_i)$. Input demands follow
from Shephard's lemma, $L_i = y_i\,\partial c_i/\partial w_i$ and
$x_{ui} = y_i\,\partial c_i/\partial p_u$. The representative household
maximizes utility given its consumption expenditure $E^h$:
\begin{equation}
\max_{c^h} \; U(c^h)
\qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad \sum_{i=1}^N p_i c_i^h \leq E^h.
\label{eq:utility-max}
\end{equation}
We take $\Phi_i$ to be a nested CES (elasticity $\epsilon$ between labour and
the intermediate composite, $\theta$ among intermediates) and $U$ to be CES
with elasticity $\sigma$. The shares of the table are its weights, so at
baseline prices the solutions to~\eqref{eq:cost-min}
and~\eqref{eq:utility-max} reproduce the table exactly. Equilibrium
requires that output satisfies intermediate and final demand in every
sector, the row identity~\eqref{eq:io-row} read as a market-clearing
condition.

These conditions determine prices and quantities only once wages,
employment and household resources are pinned down. The table and the
optimization problems are silent on both: a closure supplies them.
\endgroup

\subsection{Five labour closures and three financings}\label{subsec:closures}

Section~\ref{sec:two-questions} introduced the closures verbally;
Table~\ref{tab:closure-conditions} states the two poles and
Table~\ref{tab:closure-matrix-3x5} the full matrix, each cell by one
labour-market and one financing condition. With the kernel in place, the
symbols in these conditions can now be read off.

\paragraph{Labour-market conditions.} $L_i$ is employment in sector $i$ and
$L = \sum_i L_i$ total employment; a bar marks the baseline, so $\bar L_i$ is
baseline sectoral and $\bar L$ baseline total employment. $w$ is the common
wage of the mobile closures, $w_i$ the sector-specific wage where labour
markets are sectoral, and $\bar w$ the pegged real wage. Real wages are
deflated by the consumption price index $\Pi(p)$, the CES price of the
household's bundle, and a superscript $0$ marks baseline values.
$L_i^{\mathrm{cm}}(p, y_i, w)$ is the labour demand that solves the firm's
cost-minimization problem~\eqref{eq:cost-min} at prices $p$ and output
$y_i$. The allocation parameter $\eta$ takes only its endpoints: $\eta = 1$
lets labour move to its cost-minimizing use, $\eta = 0$ freezes the
baseline allocation. The labour-supply elasticity $\eta_{s,i}$ governs how
strongly sectoral employment responds to the real wage; the headline
results set it to a common value $\eta_s$. Finally, $\theta$, $\epsilon$
and $\sigma$ are the substitution elasticities of
Section~\ref{subsec:optimization} (among intermediates, between labour and
intermediates, and in consumption); their limit $0^{+}$ is fixed
proportions.

\paragraph{Financing conditions.} $c_i^h$ is the household's purchase of
good $i$ and $E^h$ its consumption expenditure, so $\sum_i p_i c_i^h = E^h$
is the household budget of~\eqref{eq:utility-max}, held fixed while F1
tilts its composition. $g_i$ is the programme bundle, the real public
investment in sector $i$, and $\sum_i p_i g_i$ its cost at current prices.
Under F2 that cost is raised as the programme tax $T(p)$; under F3 it is
booked as external programme financing $B_{\mathrm{gov}}$.

In words, the columns of Table~\ref{tab:closure-matrix-3x5} are as follows.

\textbf{The no-reallocation closure} fixes employment in each sector at its
baseline level and lets sector-specific wages adjust
(eq.~\ref{eq:closure-bf}). \textbf{The standard CGE closure} allows labour
to move between sectors at one flexible wage while keeping total employment
fixed (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-alpha}). \textbf{The elastic labour-supply
closure} lets sectoral employment respond to each sector's real wage,
deflated by the consumption price index, with a common supply elasticity
but separate wages (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-beta}). \textbf{The fixed real
wage closure} fixes the common real wage and lets employment adjust without
a cap (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-gamma}). \textbf{The Leontief closure} applies
the fixed-wage rule in the Leontief limit, with fixed proportions in
production and consumption (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-delta}); it is a corner
rather than a separate labour-market mechanism.

The rows of the table, the financing closures, specify how
programme-related demand is accommodated and who bears its cost. \textbf{F1} redirects household spending towards
programme sectors by changing preferences: larger spending shares there are
paid for by smaller shares elsewhere within the household budget, without
additional public purchases (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-f1}). \textbf{F2} adds
public investment financed by a household tax equal to the programme's
current-price cost (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-f2}). \textbf{F3} adds the same
public-investment bundle with external financing and no additional domestic
programme tax (eq.~\ref{eq:closure-f3}). This external financing is an
accounting counterpart, not a monetary mechanism. Under every combination
all $N$ goods markets clear and the external-account identity
holds (eq.~\ref{eq:financing-external-account}).
