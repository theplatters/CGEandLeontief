\section*{Reviewer 1}

\subsection*{R1.0 --- General assessment}
\refcom{The paper compares the IO model with two main configurations of CGEs, one
incorporating labour slack, both of which appear to embed capacity
constraints, characteristic not only of CGE models but also of other
macroeconomic models when short-run macro closures are applied. I find the
paper interesting and of interest to the reader of Metroeconomica. However I
have few points of improvements the authors would like to take into
consideration under revision stage.}
\refcom{I go straight to the main points that I believe need some restructuring.}

\response Before proceeding, let us emphasize that we agree with both reviewers that our
original narrative was somewhat ill-focused -- not only because, given parts
of our original framing, it did not spotlight the most important aspects, but
also because it led us to ignore certain foundational aspects that damaged the
credibility of the original text. Against this backdrop, we decided to rewrite
the whole draft, focusing on a more exhaustive and general treatment of our
main object of interest: the intersection between foundational assumptions of
input-output modeling and more pragmatic applications in the field of
transformational studies that rely on input-output models as a main workhorse.
While being more encompassing in terms of the model space discussed, this
fully rewritten new version allows for discussing our original question in a
more useful and better substantiated way.

In this light, we are extremely thankful for the constructive and direct
feedback received from the reviewers, which guided us in rethinking the main
setup of our narrative.
\loc{Sec.~1 (the two primitive questions, the thesis and the matrix); Sec.~3 (the closure family and Table~3c); Sec.~5 (aggregate and sectoral results).}

\subsection*{R1.1 --- Structure and central argument}
\refcom{Firstly, I find the paper challenging to follow, and its current structure
does not facilitate the reader's focus on the primary message. At this
point, I am unable to discern the central argument the authors intend to
convey.}
\refcom{What I also want to say is that differences between IO and CGE are known in
the literature, I think the authors should try to be more precise on the
message they want to sell and give an immediate focus of the paper to the
reader.}
\response The revised manuscript is motivated by an argument on the importance of input-output models for transformation research, organized around this argument rather and develops a more general inventory of models for discussing it. Section~1 states the two questions that structure the paper -- the nature of the short run and the role of money -- together with the thesis that follows from them. Section~3 turns those two questions into the closure matrix (Table~3c) that the rest of the paper works through, and Sections~5 and~6 evaluate one and the same green-investment impulse across
that matrix, first on the matrix's own terms and then once capacity and
distribution are allowed to respond. The exposition of the shared model kernel
in Section~4 is kept minimal, so that it supports the comparison rather than
preceding it.
\loc{Sec.~1; Sec.~3 (Table~3c); Secs.~5--6.}

\subsection*{R1.2 --- Verbal description of the model; static vs.\ dynamic}
\refcom{First I would like to understand the structure of the CGE model. It would be
nice to say verbally what are the main features of the model. From my
understanding, the default model is a closed economy with full employment
and a hierarchical CES function. This setup results in a fixed labour supply
and fully flexible wages, essentially representing a conventional vertical
labour supply curve. Labour is the sole primary factor of production, so
capacity constraints originate here; the same would apply if capital stock
were included and held constant.}
\refcom{Another thing should be clarified is whether the model is static or dynamic.
Here my understanding is that the authors are running a static model with
fixed capacity. Basically a short-run economy with no adjustment towards a
new-steady state.}
\response We have included a much better verbal account of the models used and also on how they connect to the key argument. For doing so, we have compartmentalized some of the formal underpinnings in our newly created appendix.
\loc{Secs.~3--4; Apps.~A and B.}

\subsection*{R1.5 --- Labor-market slack and the wage-setting rule}
\refcom{The discussion regarding labour market slack requires additional
explanation. The authors mention analysing various forms of labour market
slack, including a demand-driven CGE model and a calibrated approach. In the
demand-driven scenario, an exogenous increase in employment is introduced
based on input from the IO model. I am unsure about the rationale behind
this scenario. Even absent a model, it is apparent that GDP would rise
proportionally with employment increases, especially since price effects do
not translate into changes in competitiveness. While at the aggregate level,
differences between CGE and IO models are minimal and expected, at the
sectoral level, outcomes may vary significantly due to price sensitivity,
which depends on substitution elasticities.}
\refcom{Although I do not perceive any advantage in the demand drive scenario above
concerning labour market slack, the calibrated approach seems more
promising. However, several aspects remain unclear. This model evidently
allows for involuntary unemployment, resulting in an imperfect labour
market. Accordingly, a wage-setting rule must be specified, yet the authors
have not addressed this. Wages should no longer be determined solely by
profit maximization; instead, some wage-setting mechanism such as fixed real
or nominal wages should be implemented. Also a simple wage curve.}
\refcom{Unless I have misunderstood the authors' approach, is it simply a matter of
increasing labour supply by the number of unemployed persons, similar to the
previous scenario but with different figures?}
\response TODO: GAMMA/BETA with explicit wage rules; the no-go corollary on
nominal wage rules.

\refcom{Another aspect I observed concerns the exogenous shock. This configuration
